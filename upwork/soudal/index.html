Overview

Soudal’s team had no strong assets for their products — and a budget to make them. This was the test case: a 40-second commercial for the Multi Spray, briefed as sixteen timed lines. I directed it end to end, then rebuilt it when their technical reviewer sent a second-by-second revision deck. The team’s verdict on the July cut: “the feedback on the graphics was excellent.”

The direction

Same message.
A new look.

The starting point was an AI film a Soudal affiliate had made for the same can under another label. The brief left the door open — “if you think you can make your own video that is even better, then I’m open to that.” I kept every USP and changed the world: a dark navy studio, one hard beam, premium reflections, Soudal red as the only colour, condensed titles.

Storyboard first

Storyboards before

The sixteen lines became six storyboards in ChatGPT — 30-second and 40-second versions — then a style frame for every beat: 83 images across the project, from 136 production prompts. Only then did anything go into Google Flow to be animated.

The technical review

Seven notes.
Every one applied.

In September the client’s technical reviewer went through the July cut second by second and sent a seven-page revision deck. Six of its notes asked for changes — the closing was marked “keep” — and the team’s one negative from July was the voice. Each is shown below with the page it came from, the July frame and the same beat in the final film.

01 · Competitors · 0:02
“Remove visible competitor names/logos.”

Every can in the opening redrawn as an anonymous generic can — still clearly “the other products”, with no brand you could name.

02 · The 8 functions · 0:04
“Replace current icons with the exact 8 sell-sheet functions/icons.”

The eight approved icons, each in a glass disc, around the new line ONE SPRAY. 8 SOLUTIONS. Where the sell-sheet artwork and the brief named two functions differently, the film follows the brief.

03 · Priority functions · 0:08–0:12
“LUBRICATES · PENETRATES · REPELS WATER … add one extra scene: PREVENTS CORROSION.”

Four function beats in the client’s order of priority, each with its own shot, where July had one.

04 · 360°, once · 0:14–0:20
“Keep once … remove repeated 360° and replace with SMARTER SPRAY. MORE CONTROL.”

The 360° message now lands in one sequence; the exploded view that repeated it sells control instead.

05 · The nozzle · technical correction
“The Australian product appears to have a square profile. Please match the animation/model to the physical AU product.”

The straw in July was round and ribbed. Every head shot was rebuilt against the client’s own high-res render: a square, segmented straw on the real hinge.

=
06 · Beyond the workshop · 0:26
“Can we replace the middle image of the workshop … with outdoor use: lawnmower, shed or door lock.”

The indoor workshop became outdoor use — a lawnmower on the grass and a garden shed — while the gears, the title and the hero can stay as they were.

07 · The voice · team feedback, July
“One thing they did say was that it did sound very AI.”

The team sent three voice-overs they had used before. The new narration is slower, one line per shot, and matched to the tone of those references.

How the revision was built

Fix the frames.
Then film it again.

The July cut was made shot by shot in Google Flow. For the revision I rebuilt it as one continuous generation, so the product, the light and the pacing would hold from the first frame to the last — with the client’s own render travelling with every shot.

Decisions

Four calls that
shaped the work

None of these are on screen. All of them are why a small fix turned into a commercial, and why the revision went out in one delivery.

Passed the audition first

The first job was a PPE fix another freelancer hadn’t managed. I sent a before-and-after the next morning, then a product-swap test — and the real project followed.

Changed the look, kept the message

Rather than copy the affiliate’s film, I built a new luxury-industrial world around the same USPs. The brief had left the door open, and it became the direction the team approved.

Matched the product, not the idea of it

The review caught a nozzle that didn’t match the Australian can. Every head shot was rebuilt from the client’s own render, and that render travelled with every generation.

Threw away what drifted

I also tested a from-scratch version for the revision. When the can started to drift between frames, I dropped it and rebuilt inside the world the client had already approved.
