3RDHow a scene is re-voiced

Cut. Voice.
Sync. Mix.

A re-voiced scene fails on the smallest thing: a mouth a frame late, a voice a shade too clean, music that ducks when the line changes. Every clip went through the same six steps, shot by shot.

01

Read the script

The client’s brief gave the new lines per scene. Each line was matched to the shot, the speaker and the length of the original.

6 scenes · one brief
02

Cut the scene

From the full scene (2 to 6 minutes) down to the beats that carry the joke. Where a new line runs longer than the original, the shot is held by rocking its frames back and forth.

Up to 13 segments per scene
03

Rebuild the voice

A reference reel per character, cut from their own lines in the film, so each new line keeps the actor’s tone and rhythm.

7 reference reels · 2 min 33 s
04

Sync the mouths

Sync.so, one shot at a time, on the original footage. Takes that drifted or smeared were rendered again.

34 lip-sync renders kept
05

Keep the room

The new dialogue sits inside the original music, crowd and room tone — so the scene still sounds like the film.

Original score preserved
06

Finish for a hall

Cut and mixed in Premiere, then a second, louder master of every clip for a conference room’s speakers.

6 masters · 6 “volume up” versions

The finale · January rehire

A whole stadium,
chanting one new word

The Rudy chant, twice as hard as the rest: not one voice but a crowd, many faces on screen, and a score the client wanted kept. He asked for it done between Friday and Sunday — it was delivered on Sunday night.

One fan starts chanting “PUDDING,” slowly and rhythmically; others join one by one as the chant spreads across the stands. The entire stadium erupts chanting “PUDDING PUDDING PUDDING.”

From the crowd-sound prompt · 29 Dec

  1. Separate the score. Voices lifted out of the original mix so the music could stay under the new chant.
  2. Build the crowd. Layered voices and a generated stadium chant — one fan first, then the stands — mixed by hand.
  3. Sync many faces. Multiple passes on crowd shots, keeping the takes where every visible mouth said “Pudding”.
  4. Change the last line. The brief: “They’re chanting for Rudy!” becomes “They’re cheering for Proof of the Pudding!” — “executed exactly as I had requested.”
  5. Deliver in the window. Agreed as Friday to Sunday on his new deadline; delivered Sunday, 22:46.

7THDecisions

Four calls
behind the work

None of them are visible in a single clip. All of them are why the first contract closed in three days and the client came back.

Nº 1

Prove it before the contract

The proposal carried a working before-and-after that spoke to the client by name. The brief arrived three hours later.

Nº 2

Keep the film, change the words

No regenerated faces, no new shots. The original performance and score stayed, so the joke lands the way the scene always did.

Nº 3

Be honest about the hard part

For the chant, I explained up front that a crowd and many faces take layered passes, priced it, and still delivered inside a weekend.

Nº 4

Master for the room

A conference hall is not a laptop. Every film went out twice — the master and a louder version — so nothing got lost in the room.
