I.The brief

Photography,
not AI art

Two golf brands, one rule: the ads had to look real — no plastic skin, no fake light — and every image had to earn its place in a Meta test. “The only thing that’s really important,” Joey wrote, “is to make the ads look as real as possible.” The first product line: the Heritage Croc Leather belts, seven colours.

III.Round two · 3 February

One scene,
ten shots

To turn the chosen style into a campaign, I built GolfLens: one approved scene goes in, and a full shot list comes out — the same golfer, light and belt, re-framed as ten ad-ready photographs. Every shot could then take a new belt, a new outfit or the copy of an ad that had already sold.

The note that shaped the rest

Only one belt was used, and the same person, clothing and ad style appear throughout. Meta is likely to recognize these as essentially the same advertisement.

Joey · 3 Feb 2026

The shoot was a template; Joey was thinking like the ad auction. I rebuilt the plan around his rule in two days:

  1. Seven belts, seven worlds. Every belt gets its own ad, its own scene and its own light.
  2. An outfit for every belt. Clothing matched to each colour — some pairings will simply sell better.
  3. Different golfers, different angles. No two ads should read as the same ad to Meta.
  4. Proven words first. Headlines follow the copy that had already run for them.
IV.Rounds three and four · 5–6 February

Seven belts,
eight ads

Eight ads, each belt in at least one, each a different world: the rock by the bunker, the golfer’s desk, the putting green, the dark studio, the golfer’s waist. Built at 4K in Nano Banana Pro, finished by hand in Photoshop. Then, on the client’s note, every one went square for the feed and started saying “golf”.

The edit · 6 February

Square for the feed.
Say golf in the headline.

“…make sure customers immediately understand that this is a golf belt from the image itself.” Every headline was rewritten around the word, with the client’s list of headline ideas, and every ad re-cut to 1:1.

V.Round five · 15 February

The competitor
edition

“Ads that are performing well for competitors can be used as inspiration, but they should be recreated with our products.” Each of the 41 competitor ads was taken apart — layout, hook, type, light — and rebuilt as a Back9Lab ad with the real belt at its centre. Twenty-eight went out in one delivery.

i.

Read the ad

Layout, hierarchy, hook, angle and light, written down per ad in ChatGPT.

ii.

Write the prompt

A prompt that rebuilds the composition — never the competitor’s product or name.

iii.

Generate with our belt

Nano Banana Pro gets only the Back9Lab belt, in the right colour for the scene.

iv.

Make it true

Claims checked against the belt — no magnetic buckle, no borrowed features — and always “golf”.

VIII.Decisions

Four calls behind
the work

None of them are visible in a single ad. All of them are why the client ended with enough images to start testing, and asked for the second brand.

Nº 1

Pitch with their product

The proposal carried a finished ad for their own golf shoe, built from the website, instead of a promise. The offer came four days later.

Nº 2

Explore before committing

With no direction set, I sent 24 styles in two days. The client could point at what he wanted — and did, three times.

Nº 3

Take the Meta note seriously

When the client explained how Meta groups similar ads, the plan changed with it: two days later, eight ads covered all seven belts, each with its own golfer, outfit and world.

Nº 4

Keep every claim true

Competitor ads came with competitor features. Each rebuilt ad was checked against the real belt, and anything it doesn’t do was taken out.
