The ask

Nine 30-second header films, one per microsite page, plus a compilation.

Commercial-grade and on-brand, for a small but very high-stakes audience of PE buyers.

The constraint

Creative direction

Before Apex: desaturated, slower, stressed, legacy tech. After Apex: vibrant, faster, productive, modern. Primary blue, with fuchsia used sparingly for impact.

B-roll approach

Hybrid. AI for conceptual and architectural shots. Adobe Stock where it reads as real. Client footage for product. Counterbalance anything CGI-ish with human city and people moments.

Workflow

Build the first 30-second film as the benchmark and get it approved. Then apply the same treatment to the other eight. Assemble the compilation last, from the best beats.

Risks

AI artefacts and logo distortion: shorter shots, subtle overlays, selective grading. Lock the end card early so nine films don’t need reworking.

From storyboard to shot

Two rounds.
The first one lost.

Round one followed the storyboard to the letter, and it was genuinely what the page described. It also looked like what it was: CGI. Round two rebuilt Page 01 around grounded, human shots, and that’s the version on the site. Every raw generation is still on disk, with its original filename.

Every AI shot was eight seconds or less

Veo 3 in August 2025 generated eight-second clips, with no way to hold a logo or a character steady across them. So every page is built from short shots that each survive on their own — and that is why the sourcing was decided scene by scene rather than film by film.

The type system

Light against bold.
One magenta full stop.

Twenty-six After Effects renders, one rule. A light italic and a bold italic share every line, and the only colour on the card is the full stop. It lets a single word carry the argument — CHAOS. INSTANTLY. — without a template in sight, which was on the client’s list of don’ts.

Decisions

Four calls that
kept it on track

None of these are in the films. All of them are why the films shipped in the order they did.

Asked before building

Seven audio files, a storyboard describing four visuals per page, and two visuals per page already made. They didn’t agree. I asked two blocking questions before touching the timeline, and the scope was corrected before a single frame was cut.

Wrote the brief nobody wrote

Rather than build against a chat thread, I sent back a fourteen-section digest — deliverables, creative direction, don’ts, risks, timeline — and worked to it. Every later disagreement had a document to point at.

Threw away round one

The storyboard-literal shots were faithful and they read as CGI, which was the client’s stated worry. I reshot Page 01 around grounded, human footage instead of polishing the wrong thing.

Cleaned up the contracts

Two contracts ended up open at once. I asked to close and reopen clean so hours, milestones and the review each landed where they belonged. The client left the 5.0 before I asked.
