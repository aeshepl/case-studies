Case study Nº 07 · Overview

Before the full collection, he needed to know which roach. So we cast it: three candidates, 12 faces, 10 raw bodies, and traits that arrived 30 minutes after he asked for them.

02Candidate A · 25 Oct

Make it
cuter.

The client liked another artist’s roach, but it was metallic and read as an ant. I rebuilt it as a brown, clothes-free baseline in three hours. His partner’s verdict: too aggressive. So the same night, before touching the arms, I made twelve faces to choose from.

EXPRESSION 01 / 12

Twelve faces, one body

Same head shape, same shell, same light: only the eyes, brows, cheeks and mouth move — from smug to shy, blushing, grinning, and back to the original scowl. A face has to be locked before arms, hands and traits can be built on it.

A cuter roach arrived — wearing a helmet and clothes. An NFT baseline has to be naked, so traits can be layered on it. Ten raw-body variants went back the same afternoon: arms down, arms out, shrugging, ears or no ears, full body and bust.

RAW BODY 01 / 10

Strip it back to the shell

No helmet, no jacket, no props — just the character, so the collection can dress it later. Each variant tests one decision: how the arms sit, whether it has ears, how much of the body the NFT frame shows.

07Decisions

Four calls
behind the work

An NFT collection lives or dies on one thing — a character that stays itself across hundreds of combinations. Every decision here served that.

Nº 1

Prove it on their character

The proposal carried 29 traits generated on the client’s own sample, not a portfolio link — proof that the face survives a crown, a helmet and a new background.

Nº 2

Lock the face first

Before arms or traits, twelve expressions — because every trait inherits the face, and changing it later means redoing all sixty.

Nº 3

Ask the question that changes the set

Roach limb or human hand? One line in the delivery, but it decides how all ten hand traits get drawn.

Nº 4

Answer at the speed of the chat

Traits 30 minutes after the ask, raw bodies the same afternoon — so a client choosing between artists could decide on real images, not promises.
