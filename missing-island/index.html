The brief

He wrote the book.
He could not see it.

I don’t draw. I don’t paint. In fact, I have aphantasia, which means I have no mind’s eye. I’ve never been able to imagine anything, let alone what my characters look like.Sisan Fregene · The Cinematic TikTok Toolkit

He had already solved it once. He built his own prompt workflow, wrote it up as a 30-page toolkit, and got it working well enough to publish.

Then he hit the wall every solo creator hits with serialised visuals: volume. Twenty to twenty-five images a week, every week, all of them showing the same faces in the same clothes in rooms that kept their shape.

He did not need an artist with a style. He needed someone who could run his system at production speed without the faces drifting.

2025

Degree of difficulty

This ran on the first generation of ChatGPT image models. None of today’s conveniences existed.

There was no character reference slot, no identity lock, no style reference, no seed control and no way to pin a face between generations. Every frame was argued into place by hand: re-upload the headshot, restate the wardrobe in full, describe the room again, reject the result, say what was wrong, repeat.

A run of the same job today, on a current model, would take a fraction of the prompts. The pipeline below exists because in 2025 nothing else would hold a face still for 189 frames.

No identity lock

Faces had to be re-established from an uploaded reference on every single generation, then checked by eye afterwards.

No memory

The model forgot a wardrobe between one frame and the next, so every outfit was restated verbatim in all 25 scene beats.

No seeds

Nothing was reproducible. A frame that came out right could not be re-rolled at a new angle without starting the argument over.

How it was won

He asked for five images.
I sent a 26-page deck.

The test was unpaid: five prompts he picked himself out of Chapter 22, due end of Wednesday.

The five images were in the deck. So was everything a client cannot check by looking at a finished frame: the background prompt I started from, the mood board of failed and half-right iterations, the rewritten final prompt, the scene beat, then the delivered frame. Tool and model credited on every page.

I also told him what he could not see. That the final frames were the surface. That there were several models behind them and a lot of variations that did not make the cut. And that where he had given me no reference, like side profiles, I had made a creative assumption and was saying so out loud.

Continuity

The continuity system

Never write
“same as above”

Before a single frame of a chapter, a wardrobe pass: scan the prose for every clothing change, then write each character’s outfit out in full.

Then repeat it verbatim in all 25 scene beats. Not “as before”. Not “same outfit”. The full line, every time, because the model has no memory and an abbreviation is an invitation to improvise.

It is the least glamorous part of the job and the reason the series holds together.

Four problems

What went wrong,
and what I did

The model refused a character

Chapter 29 needed Lucy in the wardrobe the prose described, and ChatGPT blocked it on policy grounds, repeatedly. I did not send a blocker. I sent a proposed fix: a more modest outfit for this chapter, cinematic tone held, approval requested first. He picked the replacement in one line and the chapter shipped on time.

The cadence was wrong for the work

He started me on five images, then ten, reviewing each batch. Sensible for a stranger, expensive for continuity, which is decided across a whole chapter and not inside a batch of five. After the first delivery landed I asked for chapter-sized weekly milestones. He rewrote the contract into eight dated milestones that evening.

The reference pack had holes

No image guide in the Chapter 21 folder. No side profiles anywhere. No ruling on whether repeat environments should be reused directly or treated as style guides. I asked all three in one message before starting, rather than delivering 25 images built on a wrong assumption, and generated the missing side profiles myself.

Nine hours of time zone

I raised it unprompted on day one, so that “end of Wednesday” meant the same thing to both of us for the next two months. Later I asked to move the milestone week to Saturday through Thursday to match mine. He applied it to all six remaining milestones.

Eight milestones. Eight approvals. Zero revision rounds.
