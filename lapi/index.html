1The brief · 16 April

New company.
Old problem.

LA PI designs and builds kitchens and had been open four months. The owner already generated images himself on Higgsfield. What he couldn’t get was the room: everything that makes a kitchen photograph look like a place someone lives in. If the test worked, the plan was 30 images and 5–8 videos a month.

“We are looking for someone who can help us get the right type of images for our Instagram campaigns using AI.”
The client · first message, 16 April
“What I noticed that I cannot achieve for the love of god is the environment around the kitchen element. Sofas, chairs, libraries…”
The client · same evening

The paid test

  • 1One kitchen, from his own Midjourney image. The design is his.
  • 25–6 images of the same kitchen from different angles.
  • 3A film of 15 seconds or more, product-first, no people.
  • 4His brand: logos, colours and logo animations sent the next day.
  • 5Fast: “we wanted to run ads by the Tuesday max.”

3The notes · 21 April

Move the hob.
Open the room.

The same afternoon the client came back with the notes that mattered: a kitchen maker’s notes, not a designer’s. His reference had a gas hob beside the sink; no one builds that in 2026. They were fair, and they changed the kitchen itself, not just the pictures.

4Round 2 · 23–26 April

Rebuilt,
not patched.

Inpainting a new hob into sixteen old frames would have left sixteen compromises. So I started again from a new wide master of the whole room, with the island, the induction, the stools and the window, and built every frame from that. 7Behind the work

Four calls
that shaped it.

The decisions that aren’t visible in a single frame.

Nº 1

Respect the reference first

Round one kept his kitchen exactly as drawn, gas hob included. A client’s design is the client’s call; the first job was to show it from every side, not to redesign it.

Nº 2

Rebuild, don’t patch

When the layout changed, I made a new master of the whole room instead of editing the hob into old frames. It cost a round of work and bought twenty frames that agree with each other.

Nº 3

Sell the details

A kitchen is bought at arm’s length: the weight of a brass tap, the grain in a drawer, light across a marble vein. Most of the key frames are close-ups for exactly that reason.

Nº 4

Give a choice, not a guess

Five cuts with different pace, music and titles, from the same twenty frames, so the brand could hear which voice was theirs.
